Dr. Erwin Schrödinger

The man who discovered how quantum systems change over time.

Here's a time line of Dr. Schrödinger's life:

  • 1887 - Was born in Vienna, Austria, into a well-educated family that encouraged science and culture.
  • 1906 - He began studying physics and mathematics, developing a strong interest in theoretical science.
  • 1910 - Schrödinger completed his PhD in physics and started research work at the university.
  • 1914 - During World War I, he served in the Austrian army while continuing scientific work whenever possible.
  • 1921 - This period became one of the most productive times of his scientific career.
  • 1924 - He became interested in the idea that particles could behave like waves, leading him toward wave mechanics.
  • 1926 - He introduced the equation that became one of the foundations of quantum mechanics. Over several papers published that year, he developed wave mechanics and explained atomic behavior mathematically.
  • 1927 - Schrödinger joined the University of Berlin, becoming one of Europe’s leading physicists.
  • 1933 - He shared the Nobel Prize with Paul Dirac for contributions to quantum mechanics.
  • 1935 - He created the famous thought experiment to question interpretations of quantum mechanics.
  • 1939 - He joined the Dublin Institute for Advanced Studies, where he continued research in physics and philosophy.
  • 1944 - This book connected physics and biology and later influenced genetic research and molecular biology.
  • 1956 - After many years in Ireland, he returned to Austria as a professor emeritus.
  • 1961 - Erwin Schrödinger died in Vienna at the age of 73, leaving a lasting impact on modern science.
